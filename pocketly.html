The problem.

For many Gen Z users, money comes in and goes out quickly, but it is hard to see where it actually went, day to day and week to week. Without that picture, saving feels like guesswork.

01

No clear picture

Spending is hard to track across days and weeks.

02

Saving is hard

Without knowing where money goes, it is hard to hold on to it.

03

No guidance

Numbers alone don't say what to change.

Project overview.

Summary

Pocketly is a money management app that helps Gen Z users save. It tracks where they spend their money, daily and weekly, and gives suggestions on how to change their spending.

What users can do

  • See income and spending in one place.
  • Track spending daily and weekly, by category.
  • Get suggestions on how to change their money usage.
  • Open any transaction to see its details, add notes, edit or delete it.

Research & approach.

01

Personas

Started from personas of young people who earn, spend and want to save, to keep every screen focused on them.

02

Journey mapping

Mapped the journey from money coming in to money going out, to see where tracking breaks down.

03

Features

Income and expense tracking, savings goals, budgeting and visual spending summaries.

04

Mobile-first

Designed for the phone first, where young people check and log their money.

User flow

  1. 01Open the dashboard: balance, income and expenses
  2. 02See this month’s spending by category
  3. 03Open a category for its limit, breakdown and tips
  4. 04Open a transaction to see, note or edit it
  5. 05Set and track a monthly budget

Key screens.

Insights

See where your money goes

The dashboard shows the total balance with income and expenses, a donut of this month’s spending by category, and recent transactions. Open a category, like Food & Dining, to see what is left against its monthly limit, a breakdown, recent transactions and a tip such as “You’ve spent 50% of your food budget in the first half of the month.”

Transactions

Every transaction, easy to find

Transactions are easy to find and edit. After an edit, a clear confirmation shows the change is saved and reflected in the balance, with options to view the transaction or go back to the list.

Budget

Stay inside a monthly budget

A monthly budget with what is spent and what remains, and a card per category showing how much is left, with a progress bar against its limit.

Transaction details.

Design decisions.

Colour tells the story

Income shows in green; spending shows in red with a minus sign, so the direction of money is clear before reading a word.

One tag, two places

The category sits as a soft pill under the merchant and again in Details, so the same label is always in the same style.

Merchants you recognise

Each transaction leads with the merchant’s logo in a tile, which is faster to scan than text alone.

Payment at a glance

An icon next to the name shows how it was paid: account, UPI, Phone Pe or credit and debit card.

Notes for context

A short note answers “what was this for?”, like “Cab to return home” or “Coffee after work”.

Safe actions

Edit is a calm, full-width button; Delete is red text set apart below it, so it is hard to tap by mistake.
